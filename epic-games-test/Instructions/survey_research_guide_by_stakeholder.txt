PRODUCT TEAM

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. POST-PATCH / POST-UPDATE SURVEYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Measure player reaction to game updates, identify what needs fixing

SIMPLE TECHNIQUES:
- Likert Scale (1-5 satisfaction)
- Single-select multiple choice
- Open-ended text

Example 1:
"How satisfied are you with the changes in Patch v14.2?"
○ Very Dissatisfied (1)
○ Dissatisfied (2)
○ Neutral (3)
○ Satisfied (4)
○ Very Satisfied (5)

Example 2:
"Which change had the most NEGATIVE impact on your experience?"
○ Weapon balance changes
○ Map updates
○ Matchmaking changes
○ Building mechanics
○ None — all changes were positive

ADVANCED TECHNIQUES:
- MaxDiff (Maximum Difference Scaling)
- Sentiment analysis on open-text

Example 1 (MaxDiff):
"Of these patch changes, which had the MOST POSITIVE and MOST NEGATIVE impact?"
                        MOST NEGATIVE    MOST POSITIVE
Shotgun damage nerf          ○                ○
New POI additions            ○                ○
Vehicle speed buff           ○                ○
Storm circle changes         ○                ○

Example 2 (Open-text for sentiment analysis):
"In your own words, what would you change about this update?"
[Free text response — analyzed for themes and sentiment]

KEY METRICS:
- Patch Satisfaction Score (1-5 average)
- % Positive vs Negative sentiment
- Top issues ranked by MaxDiff importance score
- Theme frequency from open-text analysis

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Understand onboarding friction, improve new player retention

SIMPLE TECHNIQUES:
- Ease of use scales
- Single-select barriers
- Task completion questions

Example 1 (Ease Scale):
"How easy was it to learn how to play Fortnite?"

○ Difficult (2)

○ Easy (4)
○ Very easy (5)

"What was the most confusing part of getting started?"
○ Understanding the controls
○ Learning how to build
○ Knowing where to go on the map
○ Understanding the objective
○ Finding matches with similar skill players
○ Nothing was confusing

ADVANCED TECHNIQUES:
- Journey mapping questions
- Sequential experience rating

Example 1 (Journey Mapping):
"Rate your experience at each stage of your first session:"
                              Very Poor ←→ Excellent
Downloading/installing            1  2  3  4  5
Creating account                  1  2  3  4  5
Tutorial                          1  2  3  4  5
First match                       1  2  3  4  5
Understanding progression         1  2  3  4  5

Example 2 (Moment of Truth):
"At what point did you feel confident you understood how to play?"
○ During the tutorial
○ After 1-2 matches
○ After 5-10 matches
○ After a few days of playing
○ I still don't feel confident

KEY METRICS:
- Onboarding ease score (1-5)
- Tutorial completion rate (from behavioral data)
- Primary barrier (% selecting each)
- Time to confidence
- Day 1, Day 7, Day 30 retention (combined with behavioral)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SIMPLE TECHNIQUES:

- Demographics

○ 2-5 hours
○ 5-10 hours
○ 10-20 hours
○ More than 20 hours

ADVANCED TECHNIQUES:
- Psychographic clustering (analyzed with factor analysis)
- Latent class analysis

Example 1 (Psychographic Battery):
"How much do you agree with the following statements?" (1-5 scale)
- Winning is the most important part of gaming
- I play games mainly to socialize with friends
- I enjoy collecting rare items and completing my collection
- I like games that challenge me and help me improve
- I prefer games I can play in short sessions

KEY METRICS:
- Segment size (% of player base)
- Segment characteristics (demographics, behaviors)
- Segment value (spending, engagement)
- Segment needs/motivations
- Segment-specific satisfaction scores

================================================================================
QUICK REFERENCE: WHEN TO USE EACH ADVANCED TECHNIQUE
================================================================================

MaxDiff:
→ Use when: Prioritizing a list of 5+ items
→ Output: Importance ranking with utility scores
→ Example: "Which features matter most to players?"

Choice-Based Conjoint:
→ Use when: Optimizing multi-attribute decisions
→ Output: Part-worth utilities, optimal configuration
→ Example: "What combination of attributes creates best experience?"

Van Westendorp:
→ Use when: Finding optimal price point
→ Output: Price range (too cheap to too expensive)
→ Example: "What should we charge for this skin?"

TURF:
→ Use when: Selecting items that reach most people
→ Output: Optimal bundle/combination for maximum reach
→ Example: "Which 3 collaborations would appeal to most players?"

Kano Model:
→ Use when: Categorizing features by player expectation
→ Output: Must-have vs nice-to-have vs delighters
→ Example: "Which features are baseline expectations vs differentiators?"
