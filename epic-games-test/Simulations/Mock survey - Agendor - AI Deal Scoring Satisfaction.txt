Context & Background
- Company: Agendor (Sales-focused CRM)
- Feature: AI Deal Scoring
  - Assigns probability scores to deals (0-100%)
  - Classifies deals: High / Medium / Low close chance
  - Purpose: Help salespeople prioritize high-potential deals
- Users: Salespeople and Sales Managers
- Feature is VISIBLE to users (they know it exists and see the scores)


Research Design
Type: Evaluative / Satisfaction Study (Descriptive + Diagnostic)

Single population with internal segmentation:
- All users who have access to and have used the AI Deal Scoring feature
- Segment by: Role, Usage frequency, Experience, Deal volume

Key difference from experimental design:
- Not comparing groups with/without feature
- Measuring satisfaction and adoption within users who have the feature

Data Quality Ensurance
Rules


Quality Issue         | Detection Method              | Threshold        | Action
----------------------|-------------------------------|------------------|--------
Incomplete            | questions_answered / total    | < 50%            | Remove
Speeder               | completion_time_seconds       | < 90 sec         | Remove
Straight-liner        | variance in matrix (Q11)      | all same answer  | Flag/review
Failed attention      | attention_check_response      | ≠ expected       | Remove
Non-user              | Q4 = "Never use it"           | -                | Route to exit or abbreviated


Sample Size
* Target: 400 clean responses minimum
* For segment analysis: 100+ per key segment

Segment targets:
* Sales Reps = 250+
* Sales Managers = 100+
* Heavy users (daily/weekly) = 200+
* Light users (rarely) = 100+


Considering:
* Smaller B2B population than consumer surveys
* Higher response rates typical for SaaS (10-15%)
* Need sufficient n per segment
* Cleaning loss: ~10%


Calculation:

Target: 400 clean responses

Step 1: Add 10% cleaning buffer
        400 ÷ 0.90 = 445 raw responses

Step 2: At 12% response rate (B2B SaaS typical)
        445 ÷ 0.12 = ~3,700 invitations

Total: ~4,000 email invitations to users with AI Deal Scoring access

Statistical Analysis Summary


Method                  | Purpose                                          | Used For
------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|----------
Mean + Std Dev          | Central tendency and spread                      | All Likert questions
T2B / B2B               | Simplify Likert for communication                | Q5, Q6, Q7, Q13
Distribution %          | Understand response patterns                     | Q8, Q9, Q10, Q12, Q14
Independent t-test      | Compare 2 segments                               | Role, tenure splits
One-way ANOVA           | Compare 3+ segments                              | Deal volume, usage freq
Pearson correlation     | Relationship between variables                   | Trust-Satisfaction, etc.
Multiple regression     | Identify drivers of satisfaction                 | Attributes → Overall sat
Chi-square              | Compare distributions across segments            | Categorical outcomes
Thematic coding         | Organize open-ended responses                    | Q15, Q16


Key Relationships to Test:

1. Trust → Satisfaction (expected strong positive)
2. Trust → Behavior Change (expected strong positive)
3. Accuracy perception → Trust (expected positive)
4. Usage frequency → Satisfaction (expected positive, but check causality)
5. Tenure → Trust (does trust build over time?)
6. Deal volume → Perceived value (do high-volume users benefit more?)


Regression Model for Drivers:

Overall Satisfaction = β₀ + β₁(Accuracy) + β₂(Explanation clarity) + β₃(Score updates) 
                       + β₄(Visibility) + β₅(Workflow integration) + ε

→ β coefficients show which attributes drive overall satisfaction most

Summary

Decision Supported:
"Should we invest more in the AI Deal Scoring feature? If so, where?"


Key Findings Framework:

1. ADOPTION STATUS
   "X% of users actively use AI Deal Scoring for prioritization"
   
2. SATISFACTION LEVEL
   "Satisfaction is X/5 with Y% positive"
   
3. TRUST GAP
   "Trust (X/5) [does/does not] match perceived accuracy (Y/5)"
   
4. BEHAVIOR CHANGE
   "X% have meaningfully changed how they prioritize deals"
   
5. KEY DRIVER
   "The #1 factor driving satisfaction is [attribute]"
   
6. KEY BARRIER
   "The #1 barrier to adoption/satisfaction is [attribute]"
   
7. SEGMENT DIFFERENCES
   "[Segment] shows notably different patterns: [finding]"


Recommendation Framework:

IF satisfaction is high AND trust is high:
→ "Feature is working. Invest in promotion and deeper integration."

IF satisfaction is high BUT trust is low:
→ "Users like the concept but don't rely on it. Improve accuracy or transparency."

IF satisfaction is low AND 'explanation clarity' is the driver:
→ "Add explainability — show WHY deals get their scores."

IF satisfaction is low AND 'accuracy' is the driver:
→ "Improve the model before investing in UX."

IF segment differences exist (e.g., Managers vs Reps):
→ "Consider role-specific features or onboarding."

IF heavy users are satisfied but light users aren't:
→ "Problem is onboarding/education, not the feature itself."
