Context & Constraints
- Players do NOT know they are playing against bots
- Cannot ask directly about bots
- Must ask about match experience indirectly
- Compare groups by bot exposure INTENSITY (not just binary)
- Must control for skill level (confounding variable)
- Bot insertion is variable — players may have mixed exposure


Research Design
Type: Comparison Study with Dose-Response Analysis

Three groups from BEHAVIORAL DATA (not self-reported):
- Group A1: HIGH bot exposure (>50% of matches had bots in last 7 days)
- Group A2: LOW bot exposure (1-50% of matches had bots in last 7 days)
- Group B:  NO bot exposure (0% of matches had bots in last 7 days)

All groups receive the SAME questions about match experience.
Difference in responses = Bot impact.
Gradient across groups = Dose-response relationship.

Why Three Groups (Dose-Response):
- Bot exposure isn't binary — players have varying levels
- Allows detection of whether MORE bots = WORSE experience
- Stronger causal evidence than binary comparison
- Identifies threshold where bot exposure becomes problematic


Survey Questions
Targeting / Screening
Purpose: Identify and separate bot-exposed vs. non-bot-exposed players.
Source: Behavioral data (telemetry), NOT survey questions.


Minimum Activity Filter:
- Threshold: 10+ matches played in last 7 days
- Rationale: Ensures respondents have sufficient recent experience to evaluate
- Excludes: Tourists, lapsed players, players with insufficient exposure
- Note: ~1.4 matches/day minimum = regular player activity


Group Assignment (from backend data):

Group A1 (HIGH Bot Exposure):
- Criteria: >50% of matches had bots in last 7 days
- Minimum: 10+ total matches played
- Source: telemetry.bot_match_pct > 0.50 AND telemetry.total_matches_7d >= 10

Group A2 (LOW Bot Exposure):
- Criteria: 1-50% of matches had bots in last 7 days
- Minimum: 10+ total matches played
- Source: telemetry.bot_match_pct > 0 AND telemetry.bot_match_pct <= 0.50 
          AND telemetry.total_matches_7d >= 10

Group B (NO Bot Exposure):
- Criteria: 0% of matches had bots in last 7 days
- Minimum: 10+ total matches played
- Source: telemetry.bot_match_pct = 0 AND telemetry.total_matches_7d >= 10


Embedded Data (passed via survey URL):
- player_id
- group (A1, A2, or B)
- skill_tier (for controlling confound)
- matches_played_7d
- bot_match_percentage (exact %)

BQ1: Does bot exposure affect players' overall match experience?
Purpose: Determine if there's an overall experience difference between groups
Focus: Sentiment comparison


Survey Question Purpose: Compare overall match experience between bot-exposed and non-bot-exposed players.




Question 2: In the past 7 days, how would you rate your overall match experience in Fortnite? (single choice)
* Very negative (1)
* Negative (2)
* Neutral (3)
* Positive (4)
* Very positive (5)




Type of analysis:
* Three-group comparison (A1 vs A2 vs B)
* One-way ANOVA (overall difference)
* Post-hoc pairwise comparisons (Tukey HSD)
* Trend analysis (dose-response)
* Effect size (eta-squared for ANOVA, Cohen's d for pairwise)


Metric: 
* Mean per group
* Overall F-test significance
* Pairwise differences
* Linear trend across groups


Resolution:
"Match experience shows clear dose-response pattern:
  - Group B (no bots): 3.8
  - Group A2 (low bots): 3.3
  - Group A1 (high bots): 2.7

ANOVA: F(2, 1197) = 42.3, p < 0.001, η² = 0.07 (medium effect)
Trend: Each increase in bot exposure = -0.55 points (linear, p < 0.001)
Conclusion: More bot exposure = worse experience, in a consistent gradient."

BQ2: Does bot exposure make matches feel too easy?
Purpose: Identify if "too easy" is a symptom of bot exposure
Focus: Difficulty perception comparison


Survey Question Purpose: Compare perceived difficulty between groups without mentioning bots.




Question 3: In the past 7 days, how often did your matches feel too easy? (single choice)
* Almost never (1)
* Rarely (2)
* Sometimes (3)
* Often (4)
* Almost always (5)




Type of analysis:
* Three-group comparison (A1 vs A2 vs B)
* Chi-square test (for distribution across groups)
* One-way ANOVA (for means)
* Trend analysis


Metric: 
* % reporting "Often" or "Almost always" (Top-2-Box) per group
* Mean per group
* Linear trend


Resolution:
"'Too easy' perception shows strong dose-response:
  - Group B (no bots): 18% say 'too easy'
  - Group A2 (low bots): 41% say 'too easy'
  - Group A1 (high bots): 67% say 'too easy'

Chi-square: χ²(2) = 89.4, p < 0.001
Trend: +24.5 percentage points per exposure level (linear, p < 0.001)
Conclusion: Higher bot exposure = much more likely to perceive matches as too easy."

BQ3: Does bot exposure affect win satisfaction?
Purpose: Determine if wins against bots feel less earned
Focus: Win satisfaction comparison


Survey Question Purpose: Compare how satisfying wins feel between groups.




Question 4: In the past 7 days, how satisfying were your wins? (single choice)
* Very unsatisfying (1)
* Unsatisfying (2)
* Neutral (3)
* Satisfying (4)
* Very satisfying (5)




Type of analysis:
* Three-group comparison (A1 vs A2 vs B)
* One-way ANOVA
* Top-2-Box comparison per group
* Trend analysis


Metric: 
* Mean satisfaction score per group
* T2B (% Satisfying + Very Satisfying) per group
* Effect size (eta-squared)


Resolution:
"Win satisfaction decreases with bot exposure:
  - Group B (no bots): T2B = 71%, Mean = 4.0
  - Group A2 (low bots): T2B = 54%, Mean = 3.5
  - Group A1 (high bots): T2B = 38%, Mean = 2.9

ANOVA: F(2, 1197) = 38.7, p < 0.001, η² = 0.06 (medium effect)
Trend: -16.5 percentage points T2B per exposure level (linear, p < 0.001)
Conclusion: Higher bot exposure = less satisfying wins. Even low exposure shows meaningful reduction."

BQ4: Does the effect differ by skill level?
Purpose: Control for confounding — skill correlates with bot exposure
Focus: Segment comparison


Survey Question Purpose: No additional question needed. Analyze BQ1-BQ3 BY skill tier.




Analysis Approach:

Within EACH skill tier, compare all three groups:
* Group A1 (high bots) vs. Group A2 (low bots) vs. Group B (no bots)

If dose-response persists within skill tiers → Effect is from bots, not skill
Two-way ANOVA: Bot Exposure × Skill Tier (test for interaction)


Skill Tier Analysis Table:

Skill Tier      | A1 (High) | A2 (Low) | B (None) | Trend Sig? | Effect
----------------|-----------|----------|----------|------------|--------
New player      | 3.5       | 3.6      | 3.7      | No (p=0.31)| None
Casual          | 2.9       | 3.3      | 3.7      | Yes (p<0.01)| Medium
Regular         | 2.5       | 3.1      | 3.9      | Yes (p<0.001)| Large
Competitive     | 2.1       | 2.8      | 4.1      | Yes (p<0.001)| Very Large


Two-Way ANOVA Results:
* Main effect (Bot Exposure): F(2, 1188) = 45.2, p < 0.001
* Main effect (Skill Tier): F(3, 1188) = 12.8, p < 0.001
* Interaction (Exposure × Skill): F(6, 1188) = 8.4, p < 0.001 ← SIGNIFICANT

Resolution:
"Bot exposure effect varies significantly by skill level (interaction p < 0.001):
- New players: No meaningful difference across exposure levels
- Casual/Regular: Moderate dose-response effect
- Competitive: Strongest effect — high-bot players score 2.0 points lower than no-bot players

Key insight: The dose-response pattern is strongest for skilled players. Even LOW bot exposure hurts competitive players (-1.3 vs no bots)."

BQ5: Does bot exposure affect intent to continue playing?
Purpose: Assess urgency — is this a retention risk?
Focus: Intent comparison


Survey Question Purpose: Compare churn risk between groups.




Question 5: How likely are you to continue playing Fortnite in the next month? (single choice)
* Definitely will not (1)
* Probably will not (2)
* Might or might not (3)
* Probably will (4)
* Definitely will (5)




Type of analysis:
* Three-group comparison (A1 vs A2 vs B)
* One-way ANOVA and T2B comparison
* Segment by skill tier
* Trend analysis


Metric: 
* Continue intent T2B (% Probably + Definitely) per group
* B2B (% at risk) per group
* Trend significance


Resolution:
"Continue intent shows dose-response pattern:
  - Group B (no bots): T2B = 84%, At-risk = 8%
  - Group A2 (low bots): T2B = 77%, At-risk = 12%
  - Group A1 (high bots): T2B = 68%, At-risk = 19%

By skill tier (A1 high-bot vs B no-bot):
  - New players: 82% vs 85% (minimal difference)
  - Casual: 72% vs 83% (-11 points)
  - Regular: 65% vs 84% (-19 points)
  - Competitive: 54% vs 91% (-37 points) ← Significant churn risk

Conclusion: High bot exposure creates meaningful churn risk, especially for competitive players where nearly half are at-risk."

Sample Size
* Rule of thumb: 400 responses per group ensures statistical power to detect medium effects in ANOVA

Minimum per group:
* Group A1 (High bot exposure) = 400
* Group A2 (Low bot exposure) = 400
* Group B (No bots) = 400
* Per skill tier per group = 100 (minimum for segment analysis)


Considering:
* Three-group ANOVA requires slightly less per group than two-group t-test
* Need to detect effect size f ≥ 0.25 with 80% power
* Must have sufficient n within each skill tier for interaction analysis
* Cleaning loss: ~15%
* Response rate: ~6% (email)


Calculation:

Target: 400 clean responses per group × 3 groups = 1,200 total
+ Per skill tier (4 tiers × 100 minimum) = 400 per group needed
→ Target 400 per group minimum

Step 1: Clean responses needed
        400 × 3 groups = 1,200 clean total

Step 2: Add 15% cleaning buffer
        1,200 ÷ 0.85 = 1,412 raw responses

Step 3: Email send volume (at 6% response rate)
        1,412 ÷ 0.06 = ~24,000 emails

Split by group:
        Group A1: 8,000 emails → ~480 responses → ~400 clean
        Group A2: 8,000 emails → ~480 responses → ~400 clean
        Group B:  8,000 emails → ~480 responses → ~400 clean

Total: ~24,000 emails sent, targeting 1,200 clean responses


Population Size Consideration:
- Group A2 (low exposure, 1-50%) is likely the LARGEST group
- Group B (no bots, 0%) may be RARE for lower-skill players
- Group A1 (high exposure, >50%) varies by skill tier

May need to oversample Group B if population is small:
- Check population distribution before sending
- Adjust send volume per group based on availability




Representativeness Ensurance


1. Groups must be comparable
   - Match on: region, platform, total play time
   - Control for: skill tier (the known confound)

2. Within-group representativeness
   - Each group should mirror Fortnite population
   - Check: platform, region, play frequency

3. Sample balance check
   - Compare Group A vs Group B on demographics
   - If imbalanced → weight or note limitation

4. Mid-Survey monitoring
   - Track response rates by group
   - Send reminders if one group lags

Statistical Rigor Requirements


For Three-Group Comparison Research:


Metric              | Requirement           | Why
--------------------|----------------------|--------------------------------
Sample size/group   | n ≥ 400              | Power to detect medium effects
Overall test        | One-way ANOVA        | Test if ANY group differs
Post-hoc tests      | Tukey HSD            | Which pairs differ?
Trend analysis      | Linear contrast      | Is there dose-response?
Effect size         | Report eta-squared   | Overall effect magnitude
Pairwise effect     | Report Cohen's d     | Specific comparison magnitude
Power               | ≥ 80%                | Likely to detect effect if exists
Confound control    | Two-way ANOVA        | Test Exposure × Skill interaction


Key Statistical Tests:

1. ANOVA (One-Way)
   - Tests: Do the three groups differ overall?
   - Report: F(df1, df2) = X, p = Y, η² = Z

2. Linear Trend
   - Tests: Is there a consistent gradient (more bots = worse)?
   - Report: Linear contrast F = X, p = Y

3. Post-Hoc Pairwise (Tukey HSD)
   - Tests: Which specific pairs differ?
   - Report: A1 vs B: diff = X, p = Y, d = Z

4. Two-Way ANOVA (with Skill Tier)
   - Tests: Does the effect vary by skill?
   - Report: Interaction F = X, p = Y


Report Format:
"Three-group comparison: A1 = X, A2 = Y, B = Z
 ANOVA: F(2, n) = [value], p = [value], η² = [value]
 Linear trend: p = [value]
 Post-hoc A1 vs B: diff = [value], p = [value], d = [value]"

Summary

Decision Supported:
"Should we make bots more difficult?"


Key Findings Framework:

1. DOSE-RESPONSE PATTERN
   "More bot exposure [does/does not] correlate with worse experience"
   
2. THRESHOLD IDENTIFICATION
   "Negative impact starts at [low / high] exposure levels"
   
3. SPECIFIC SYMPTOMS
   "Higher-exposure players report matches feel [too easy / less satisfying]"
   
4. SKILL MODERATION
   "The dose-response effect is [consistent across / varies by] skill level"
   
5. URGENCY
   "High bot exposure [does/does not] create meaningful churn risk"


Recommendation Framework:

IF clear dose-response exists (A1 < A2 < B):
→ "Reduce overall bot frequency in matchmaking — each added bot hurts experience"

IF threshold exists (A1 ≠ B but A2 ≈ B):
→ "Low bot exposure is acceptable; focus on preventing high-exposure matches"

IF effect is concentrated in high-skill players:
→ "Reduce bot frequency for experienced players; maintain current for new players"

IF no dose-response (A1 ≈ A2 ≈ B):
→ "Bot exposure level doesn't matter — look at other factors"

IF new players benefit but dose-response hurts experienced:
→ "Implement skill-based bot frequency caps (fewer bots as skill increases)"
